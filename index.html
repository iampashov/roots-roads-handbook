About the project

Roots to stand on, roads to walk together

“Roots & Roads: Inclusion of Roma, Migrant and Refugee Youth” is a youth exchange bringing together 36 young people from 6 partner countries, hosted in Bansko, Bulgaria, from 2 to 11 November 2026.

The project responds to the ongoing challenges of discrimination, social exclusion and unequal participation faced by Roma, migrant and refugee youth across Europe. The exchange strengthens active citizenship, intercultural competences and anti-discrimination skills through shared learning and cooperation.

Using non-formal learning methods, participants take part in Living Library sessions, intercultural workshops, community mapping, forum theatre and creative activities, and they build real friendships across borders.

The programme

Erasmus+ & Youthpass

What the programme behind this exchange actually is, and the certificate every participant takes home.

Erasmus+ Youth

Erasmus+ is the EU programme for education, training, youth and sport. Its youth strand funds learning mobility and cooperation so young people can grow, meet across borders and take part in society.

KA152 Youth Exchange

Roots & Roads is a KA152 Youth Exchange: short group mobility where young people from several countries live and learn together through non-formal activities around a shared theme.

Youthpass

Every participant receives a Youthpass, a European certificate that describes what they learned and makes non-formal learning visible for study, work and life.

Eight key competences

Youthpass is built on the eight key competences for lifelong learning, from multilingual and personal and social competence to cultural awareness and citizenship.

Why we do it

Project objectives

01

Empower young people

Empower Roma, migrant and refugee youth to participate actively in their communities and civic life.

02

Build intercultural competences

Develop intercultural competences and strengthen understanding between young people from different cultural and social backgrounds.

How we work

Methods for inclusion & anti-racism

The non-formal approach behind every activity.

Non-formal learning

Learning by doing and reflecting, in a relaxed setting where everyone takes part and nobody is graded.

A safe space & group agreement

The group writes its own rules together so everyone can speak, disagree with respect and opt out of anything too personal.

Start from lived experience

Activities begin from real life and community, not from theory, and no one has to speak for a whole group.

Challenge the behaviour, not the person

We name exclusion and stereotypes and practise interrupting them, while keeping respect for every person in the room.

Peer learning & reflection

Daily reflection groups and Youthpass turn the experience into learning each person can name and reuse.

Creative and embodied methods

Theatre, film, mapping and storytelling let people explore inclusion with their whole selves, not only with words.

Challenge the behaviour, not the person.

We name exclusion and stereotypes out loud, and we keep respect for every single person in the room.

Know the enemy

Stereotypes & prejudice

A short, shared language for the things this project works against.

01

What is a stereotype

A fixed, oversimplified picture of a whole group ("they are all…"). It is a mental shortcut that ignores the real, different people inside the group.

02

What is prejudice

A judgement or feeling about someone because of the group they belong to, formed before you actually know them.

03

What is discrimination

When a stereotype or prejudice turns into action: treating someone worse, leaving them out, or denying them something because of who they are.

04

How it grows

Stereotype leads to prejudice, prejudice leads to discrimination, and unchecked discrimination can harden into exclusion and even violence. It can be stopped at any step.

05

Antigypsyism & racism

Specific, long-standing prejudice against Roma, and racism against migrants and refugees, remain widespread in Europe. Naming them is the first step to ending them.

06

How to interrupt it

Meet the person behind the label, ask instead of assume, speak up as a bystander, and share real stories that break the stereotype.

About Falken (FBF Hagen)

Falken Bildungs- und Freizeitwerk Hagen e.V.

FBF Hagen has decades of experience in non-formal youth education and community development. Its core staff and many volunteers are professionally trained youth educators and social workers.

The FBF is well integrated into Hagen’s social infrastructure, working with schools, municipal agencies and migrant self-organisations. Over the years the Falken have run countless local projects, from educational workshops to cultural festivals, often with a focus on diversity and social justice.

FBF has taken part in multiple Erasmus+ initiatives, bringing international expertise back to the local community, and combines grassroots experience through its three youth centres with EU project-management competence.
